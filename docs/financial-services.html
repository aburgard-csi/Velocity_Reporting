Start here · 01

What this is, and what you do

Financial Services is the part of the Transaction Intelligence deck that answers one question: where do our members and customers send money that is not to us? It reads the debit-card transaction files, finds the payments that go to someone else's financial product (an auto loan, a credit card, insurance, a buy-now-pay-later plan) and tells you how many accounts, how many dollars, how recent, and who they pay.

It is not the same as Competition. Competition is banks and credit unions (who holds the relationship). Financial Services is the product (what they are buying). The same payment can appear in both, and the two views are never added together; see How it relates to Competition.

What you do with it

You read the numbers (Read the numbers), open the mail lists (The lists and the master list) and give the client's marketing team the master list. If a client's numbers look thin, the coverage line says how much the dictionary could see, and the review queue says what to add.

Everything on this page is read out of the code when it is built, so it cannot describe a figure the pipeline no longer computes. If a number here looks wrong, the code moved: rebuild the page.

Start here · 02

Read the numbers

Three words are used on every slide and in every list. Learn them once.

WordMeansUse it for
Any leakageThe account paid at least one outside financial product, in any of the 20 leakage categories.The headline: how many members or customers have a financial life elsewhere.
AddressableThe account paid one of the 17 categories the institution offers (auto loans, mortgage, cards, personal loans, student loans, and others; not insurance or tax).What the institution could realistically win back.
Adjacency2 categories: person-to-person payments and fintech wallets. Reported apart, never added to leakage.Context, not a leak: a Venmo payment may be rent.
Two percentages, one client

On 1798 the headline reads 42% (any leakage) and the by-lens table reads 18% (addressable). Both are right; they answer different questions. Every figure in the pack now carries both, with the base printed (fin_definitions), so quote them together.

Every figure is a floor

The files are debit-card rows. Payments made by ACH, check or a bill-pay service are not in them, so every account and dollar figure is the least that is true. The slides say “card rails only” for this reason. Wallet share (the category's share of an account's ACH and check dollars) needs those rails and is blank on a card-only file; the coverage line says so.

Dollars are payments, not balances

A $50M figure is money that moved in twelve months. It is not a loan book and carries no rate or margin. No revenue-at-stake estimate is made, by decision.

Get running · 03

What it needs

Nothing beyond what a Transaction Intelligence build already reads:

  • The ODD extract for the month (the account list, branch, product, Mailable?, business or personal).
  • The monthly debit-card transaction files for the client (Inputs\Txn Files\<id>). Twelve months is the full picture; a single month works but has no trend.
  • The client's config (ti_clients_config.json): SelfNames (the client's own names, so internal transfers are never leakage) and, if the client has providers the dictionary lacks, LocalFinancialServices.

A client with no transaction files has no Financial Services chapter, and the build says so instead of drawing empty slides.

Use it · 06

The lists and the master list

Every category gets a list, and there is one master list across all of them, so a campaign is a filter, not a rebuild.

ColumnWhat it is
acct_idThe account, as the ODD and the transaction file key it (a long hashed id, not an account number).
cifThe member's customer information file number. Blank today: no client file carries one. It fills when a crosswalk is supplied; nothing else changes.
list_typeThe category, which is the campaign or list type (Auto Loans, Credit Cards, …).
kindleakage or adjacency.
addressableyes if the institution offers this category.
tierHow recently the account last paid: active (30 days), cooling (90), dormant (a year), lapsed.
txnsHow many payments in the window.
dollarsHow much, in the window.
wallet_shareThat category's share of the account's ACH and check dollars; blank without the transfer rail.
lastThe date of the last payment.
mailableThe ODD's Mailable? flag, or unknown for an account the ODD does not list.

The master list's columns, in order: acct_id, cif, list_type, kind, addressable, tier, txns, dollars, wallet_share, last, mailable.

How to use it

  1. Open the master list and filter mailable to Yes. An account marked unknown is not in this month's ODD: leave it out.

  2. Pick the campaign by list_type and addressable. Start with cooling: those accounts paid in the last 31 to 90 days and are the cheapest to keep.

  3. Add the CIF from the institution's own system when marketing needs it; the file carries the account id only.

Business accounts

The lists cover the personal-account base, the same accounts the headline counts. Business accounts are not in them.

Mailing is not Competition

These lists are financial-services lists only. Competition keeps its own files, and competition-based mail targeting is not part of this work.

Coverage: how much the dictionary can see

The dictionary matches names, so it can only count what it knows. Every build reports it, in the build log and in the pack (fin_coverage):

  • Matched rows and dollars as a share of the file. Most card rows are ordinary shopping, so the row share is low by design; look at dollars.
  • Review queue dollars: money that went to something financial-looking the dictionary did not recognise. This is the number to bring down.
  • Wallet share available: False on a card-only file.

On 1798's October 2026 file, the first run matched 15.9% of dollars and left $1.14M in the review queue; adding the names from that queue moved 1,765 rows into categories.

How it relates to Competition

Competition counts who; Financial Services counts what. A payment to a named bank or credit union is in both:

The rule

NUSENDA CU AUTO LOAN is an auto loan here and Nusenda in Competition. Each view counts it. The two are never added. Where the page or a slide shows both, it says the other view exists.

fininst_by_category and fininst_matrix are the bridge: for each category, how much sits at a named bank or credit union, and the largest pairs with their tier. They are cross-tabs of the two views, not a third total.

When the Competition layers run, the institution is named by the registry (every FDIC bank and NCUA credit union, matched on the card description, with its tier: National, Digital, Local, Regional, State, Other, or a card-issuing bank). A name that fits several institutions is left unplaced, never guessed. Without the layers (no reference store), the match falls back to the institutions Competition is configured with, and a depository in neither is not found. fininst_source says which was used. Either way the share is a floor, because the files are card rows.

Know the limits · 12

What it cannot do

  • See payments off the card rail. ACH, checks and bill pay are not in the files. Every figure is a floor.
  • Know a loan balance, rate or margin. It sees payments. It does not estimate revenue or opportunity.
  • Name a lender that is not in the dictionary. It shows in the review queue until someone adds it.
  • Tell you who an account is. No client file carries a name, address or CIF. The lists key on the account id.
  • Prove a program effect. The responder-against-control gap is descriptive: responders are chosen by a dip in activity. Only a before-and-after comparison against a control is causal.
  • Cover business accounts in the lists. The headline and the lists are personal accounts.

When the numbers look wrong

You seeIt meansDo this
No Financial Services slidesNo transaction files for the period, or the chapter failed.Read the build log for failed: or not on hand. The ledger lists the slides as hidden with the reason.
A provider shows as (unnamed)The descriptor names a product but no lender (LOAN PMT).Normal. If it is a big dollar amount, look it up in the review queue.
A large review queueFinancial-looking counterparties the dictionary does not know.Open finserv_review_queue.csv, take the top names, and add them (below).
A bank or credit union in the review queueIt is a Competition name, not a product.Add it to the client's LocalCompetitors (Competition's config), not to this dictionary.
Wallet share is blankThe file has no ACH or check rows.Expected for a card-only client.
A category list is emptyNobody paid that category this month.The file is still written, header only, so the set of files never changes.

Adding a name to the dictionary

  1. Read the review queue and pick names that are real lenders, insurers or wallets. Leave out look-alikes (a store called “Sun Valley Hardware” is not Sun Loans).

  2. One client only? Put the name under LocalFinancialServices in that client's config. Every client? Add it to the category in financial_services_patterns.json, once. A pattern in two categories fails the tests.

  3. Change goes through a branch and pull request, with the before and after for a real client. A dictionary change moves figures, so it is its own PR.

Reference · 14

Wording on slides and lists

Credit union: say member (members, member accounts, members at GECU). Bank: say customer. Text that covers both: “members and customers”, or the institution's own word per row. Never “user”, “consumer” or “account holder”.

Glossary

Leakage
Money to someone else's financial product (loan, card, insurance, brokerage, BNPL, crypto, payday).
Addressable
A leakage category the institution offers.
Adjacency
Person-to-person and fintech wallet payments, reported apart.
Review queue
Financial-looking descriptors the dictionary did not match, by dollars.
Wallet share
A category's share of an account's ACH and check dollars; needs the transfer rail.
Coverage
How much of the file the dictionary can see.
CIF
The member or customer's customer information file number. Not in any file today.
Master list
One row per account per category; the single hand-off to marketing.
Ledger
A line for every slide with its status and reason.
ODD
The monthly account-level extract.
Lens
A program group: for ARS, responders, mailed non-responders and no mail; for ICS, ICS accounts and their sources.
